# 12-Week Sprint Triathlon Training Plan
## Beginner to Sprint Ready

**Race Distance:** 750m Swim | 20km Bike | 5km Run  
**Target Audience:** Beginners able to swim 15 min, bike 30 min, run 30 min  
**Weekly Training:** 5-6 days per week | 4-6 hours total  
**Program Structure:** 3 x 4-week cycles (Test → Build → Recover)

---

## Program Overview

This 12-week plan uses proven periodization principles to progressively build your fitness across all three disciplines. Each 4-week cycle includes a testing phase to establish baseline fitness, a build phase to increase volume and intensity, and a recovery week to allow adaptation.

**Key Principles:**
- Progressive overload: Gradual increases in volume and intensity
- Specificity: Training mimics race demands
- Recovery: Built-in rest days and recovery weeks
- Flexibility: Adjust rest days to fit your schedule

## CYCLE 1: BASE BUILDING (Weeks 1-4)

### Week 1: Test Week

**Monday:** Rest day - Meal prep and schedule planning

**Tuesday:** Swim Test (30 min)
- Warm-up: 5-10 min easy swim
- Main Set: Swim 15 min max distance (take breaks as needed)
- Cool-down: 5 min easy swim
- *Note: Record distance for baseline*

**Wednesday:** Easy Bike (45 min)
- Entire session easy/conversational pace
- Use easy gear with high cadence (90+ RPM)
- Focus on smooth pedaling, not speed

**Thursday:** Run Test (45 min)
- Warm-up: 10 min easy walk/jog
- Main Set: Run/walk 30 min max distance
- Cool-down: 5 min easy walk
- *Note: Record distance for baseline*

**Friday:** Easy Swim (20 min)
- Swim easy, take breaks as needed
- Focus on form and breathing

**Saturday:** Bike Test (45 min)
- Warm-up: 10 min easy
- Main Set: Bike 30 min max distance
- Cool-down: 5 min easy
- *Note: Record distance for baseline*

**Sunday:** Easy Run (30 min)
- Run/walk easy (conversational pace)
- Take breaks as needed

**Weekly Volume:** ~4 hours

## Training Terminology

**Test Pace (TP):** The pace you established during your test weeks. Maintain this effort level during interval workouts.

**Easy/Conversational Pace:** You should be able to hold a conversation. If you can't speak, you're going too fast.

**Warm-up (WU):** Gradually increase heart rate and blood flow. Never start hard.

**Main Set (MS):** The primary focus of the workout.

**Cool-down (CD):** Gradually decrease intensity to aid recovery.

**Recovery Interval (RI):** Easy effort between hard efforts to allow partial recovery.

## Nutrition During Training

**Pre-Workout (30-60 min before):**
- Banana with almond butter
- Toast with jam
- Rice cakes with honey
- *Something light and easily digestible*

**During Workout (sessions >90 min):**
- Water with electrolytes
- Energy gels or sports drink
- *Not needed for most sessions in this plan*

**Post-Workout (within 30 min):**
- Protein + carbs (chicken with rice, protein smoothie, turkey sandwich)
- Rehydrate with electrolytes
- *Critical for recovery*

---

## Recovery & Injury Prevention

**Sleep:** Aim for 7-9 hours per night. This is when adaptation happens.

**Stretching:** 10-15 min after each workout, especially legs and shoulders.

**Foam Rolling:** 5-10 min on rest days for muscle recovery.

**Cross-Training:** Optional easy yoga or swimming on rest days.

**Listen to Your Body:** If something hurts (not just tired), take an extra rest day.

---

## Race Week Tips

- Reduce training volume by 50-70%
- Maintain intensity but reduce duration
- Practice your race-day routine
- Lay out all gear the night before
- Get good sleep the 2-3 nights before
- Eat familiar foods
- Arrive at race venue early for parking and setup

---

## Post-Race

Congratulations on completing your first sprint triathlon! Now:
- Rest for 3-5 days with easy movement only
- Reflect on what went well and what to improve
- Consider your next goal (another sprint, Olympic distance, or half ironman)
- Celebrate your achievement!

---

**Remember:** This is YOUR training plan. Adjust rest days as needed to fit your life. Consistency beats perfection. Trust the process!
