# Half Marathon Training Plan
## 12-Week Beginner Program

**Race Distance:** 21.1 kilometers (13.1 miles)  
**Target Audience:** Runners with 5K experience or basic running fitness  
**Prerequisites:** Able to run 5K continuously or have completed 5K training program  
**Weekly Training:** 4-5 days per week | 4-6 hours total  
**Program Structure:** 3 cycles with progressive long runs and speed work

---

## Program Overview

This 12-week program takes runners from 5K fitness to half marathon ready. The plan introduces long runs, tempo runs, and speed work to build endurance and race-specific fitness. By race day, you'll have completed a 20K long run and be confident crossing the finish line.

**Key Principles:**
- Progressive long run increases (no more than 10% per week)
- One speed/tempo session per week for race pace development
- One easy run and one moderate run per week
- Strategic taper in final weeks
- Injury prevention through proper recovery and cross-training

## Running Pacing Guidelines

**Easy Pace:** You should be able to hold a conversation comfortably. This is your primary training pace.

**Tempo Pace:** Comfortably hard. You can speak a few words but not full sentences. Approximately 25-30 seconds per mile slower than race pace.

**Race Pace:** The pace you plan to run the half marathon. You can speak in short sentences but not full conversations.

**Speed Work Pace:** 800m repeats should be at approximately 5K race pace or slightly faster.

## Nutrition for Half Marathon Training

**Pre-Run (1-2 hours before):**
- Banana with almond butter
- Toast with jam
- Rice cakes with honey
- Small bowl of oatmeal

**During Run (sessions >60 min):**
- Water with electrolytes every 15-20 min
- Sports drink or energy gels every 30-45 min
- Aim for 30-60g carbs per hour

**Post-Run (within 30 min):**
- Protein + carbs (chicken with rice, protein smoothie, turkey sandwich)
- Rehydrate with water or electrolyte drink
- Aim for 3:1 carb to protein ratio

## Injury Prevention

**Warm-up:** Always start with 5 minutes of easy walking before running.

**Cool-down:** Always end with 5 minutes of easy walking after running.

**Stretching:** Stretch for 15-20 minutes after each run, focusing on calves, hamstrings, quads, hip flexors, and IT band.

**Cross-Training:** Consider one day of cross-training (cycling, swimming, strength training) per week for injury prevention.

**Rest Days:** Take at least 2-3 complete rest days per week. Your body adapts during rest, not during training.

**Listen to Your Body:** If you experience sharp pain (not just muscle soreness), take an extra rest day or consult a healthcare provider.
